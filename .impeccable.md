## Design Context

### Users
Design-system designers and design engineers working alongside Figma. They use the app to map component → variant → token relationships and share the plan with their team. They are comfortable with design-tool conventions (canvas, layers, variants, tokens) but are not necessarily developers.

### Brand Personality
Precise, Figma-adjacent, professional. The interface should feel like a credible design-system tool: calm, confident, and utilitarian, with moments of vibrancy from the per-type component colors. It should not feel playful, toy-like, or decorative.

### Aesthetic Direction
Light and dark themes. Near-white / near-black neutrals with a vibrant per-type palette (purple main, cyan variant, green sub, yellow token, orange instance) and a cyan accent. Typography pairs Unbounded (display) with Inter (body) and DM Mono (mono). Rounded, soft surfaces; a floating Figma-style dock; subtle spring easing. Anti-references: generic AI dashboards, glassmorphism, gradient text, hero-metric layouts.

### Design Principles
1. **The canvas is the product.** Panels and chrome should recede so the diagram stays the focus.
2. **Precision over decoration.** Every control earns its place; no ornamental gradients, glows, or card-within-card nesting.
3. **Figma-adjacent, not Figma-cloned.** Borrow the interaction model (dock, canvas, layers) without copying its visual identity.
4. **Progressive disclosure.** Keep the resting state quiet; reveal detail on hover, selection, or explicit expansion.
5. **Color carries meaning.** Component-type colors are semantic, not decorative; never use them as generic accents.
